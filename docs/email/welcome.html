Kilroy quietly tells you when the emails you send get opened. Here's how to use it, what to trust, and how your data stays yours — the parts worth knowing that the extension can't fit next to a button.

Using it
1 Compose a message in Gmail — a Tracking chip appears next to Send.
2 Send it. When it's opened, a ✓✓ with a count appears on your sent message, right inside the thread.
3 Open the dashboard from the Kilroy icon for opens per day, opens by recipient, and every message you've tracked.
What an "open" really means

An open is an image being fetched — not proof a person read your email. Kilroy sets aside the things that fetch images but aren't people: security scanners, Apple Mail's privacy prefetch, and your own re-reads. It never invents a city or a device. Treat the first-open time as the reliable number; counts are a floor, not a total.

Your data is yours

Your tracking is scoped to your account and isolated from every other user — no one else can see it. Want to hold it entirely yourself? One click in the options points Kilroy at your own database, and then nobody, not even us, can read it.

Good to know
• Turn it off per message. Click the chip beside Send to not track a particular email.
• Pin Kilroy — click the puzzle-piece icon in Chrome's toolbar, then the pin next to Kilroy, so the dashboard is always one click away.
• Using your own database? Export your settings (Options → Preferences) — a reinstall wipes them, and the file is the only way back.
Open Gmail and try it

Kilroy is open source — read every line at github.com/RelayLabs-apps/kilroy. Open tracking sits in a genuine grey area; use it on your own correspondence, at your own discretion.
